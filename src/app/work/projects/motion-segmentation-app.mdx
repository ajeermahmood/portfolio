## Overview

Ascenda is a Nestlé nutrition product for children, and its pitch is height. For the launch to healthcare professionals at the Ritz-Carlton in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, the brief was to let guests feel that pitch rather than read it. I built two pieces for the ballroom, delivered through NTAM Group.

- **A growth wall.** A webcam and a wall-sized LED screen. A guest steps in front of it, the app tracks their body, and on screen they stretch taller as the Ascenda "effect" kicks in. They capture the moment and print it or share it on the spot.
- **A running game.** A Mario-inspired side-scroller, also built in Electron, on three kiosks and played with an Xbox controller. The runner grows taller by collecting Ascenda and shrinks when hit by sugar molecules, which is the product's message turned into a game mechanic.

More than 150 guests used the two pieces over the evening.

## The growth wall

It is a desktop application that reads the webcam, tracks the person in front of it and separates them from the background, so the ballroom behind them is replaced by the campaign artwork and their body can be stretched without the wall stretching with them.

- **Full-body tracking.** Pose, hands and face through MediaPipe Holistic, so the growth animation follows the actual person and the capture triggers on a raised arm rather than on a click.
- **Background replacement.** Segmentation separates the subject from whatever is actually behind them, and the campaign artwork takes its place.
- **Capture, print and share.** A still of the finished frame goes to a printer at the stand and to a QR code for the guest's phone.
- **Windows and Mac.** One Electron build covering both, because the venue hardware was not known until the week before.

## Why a desktop app

This runs at an event on hardware someone else set up. It needs a camera, predictable frame rate and no dependency on the venue's network. A browser tab is the wrong shape for that. Electron gives it direct access to the camera and a fixed runtime, so behaviour on the night matches behaviour in testing.

Redux carries the tracking state, which changes many times a second and is read by several parts of the interface at once. Keeping that in one place rather than threading it through components is what keeps the frame rate usable on a 4 metre screen.

## The game

The runner is a second Electron app, a Mario-inspired side-scroller I wrote from scratch for the three kiosks. The character grows taller by collecting Ascenda and shrinks when hit by sugar molecules, and the only input is an Xbox controller read through the browser's Gamepad API inside the Electron shell.

It was built for a kiosk, which changes the design more than it sounds. There is no keyboard, the session has to reset itself when a child walks away mid-run, and a queue is forming behind every player. So each run is short, the controller is the only input, and the attract screen comes back on its own after a few seconds of nothing happening.

## What I would do differently

Test on the real screen earlier. The tracking was tuned on a laptop, and a 4 metre LED wall with stage lighting behind the guest is a different segmentation problem. It held up, but the first hour at the venue went on lighting rather than on the app.
